<header class="script-header"><div class="reading-shell"><p class="eyebrow">Seven-minute presentation</p><h1>ABD-Net <span class="gradient-text">showcase script</span></h1><p class="lead">A complete talk track from the research paper through implementation, diffusion-policy imitation learning, and evaluation.</p><div class="notice"><strong>Presentation note:</strong> Text under “Show” is a visual cue and should not be spoken. Text under “Say” is the narration.</div></div></header>

<section class="section-compact"><div class="reading-shell"><div class="script-list">
<article class="script-step"><span class="script-time">0:00–0:35</span><h2>Hook</h2><div class="show-cue"><strong>Show:</strong> A RollBall evaluation video, followed by the robot’s kinematic tree.</div><blockquote class="script-copy">Robot policies normally learn physics indirectly from data. My project asks whether part of that burden can be removed by placing the computational structure of robot dynamics inside the network. I started from ABD-Net, built it as a working graph policy, and then extended it beyond the paper—from reinforcement learning to diffusion-based imitation learning.</blockquote></article>
<article class="script-step"><span class="script-time">0:35–1:25</span><h2>The paper’s central idea</h2><div class="show-cue"><strong>Show:</strong> The original paper and the Φ → M → Ψ architecture.</div><blockquote class="script-copy">A standard graph network knows which robot links are connected, but its aggregation rule has no physical meaning. ABD-Net goes further. It mirrors the Articulated Body Algorithm, where inertial effects accumulate from child links toward the root.<br><br>Each link gets its own observation encoder. Each child sends a dynamics-inspired message to its parent. Finally, each joint action is decoded from the resulting link representation. The network remains learnable, but its information flow begins with a useful physical prior.</blockquote></article>
<article class="script-step"><span class="script-time">1:25–2:25</span><h2>Turning the equations into code</h2><div class="show-cue"><strong>Show:</strong> Graph construction and the DynamicsMessagePassing class.</div><blockquote class="script-copy">I first converted the robot articulation from SAPIEN into a DGL graph. Its edges point from child to parent so DGL can perform a leaf-to-root topological pass.<br><br>I stored actuator order explicitly because graph traversal order is not necessarily the action order expected by the simulator. Without this mapping, a structurally correct network could still control the wrong joints.<br><br>I then implemented the paper’s three main modules: per-link encoders, dynamics-informed propagation, and per-joint action heads. I also implemented the weighted orthogonality loss and introduced a smooth bounded projection to improve numerical stability in deeper kinematic trees.</blockquote></article>
<article class="script-step"><span class="script-time">2:25–3:15</span><h2>Validating the implementation with PPO</h2><div class="show-cue"><strong>Show:</strong> PPO training code, Hopper or Humanoid checkpoints, metrics, and an evaluation video.</div><blockquote class="script-copy">I integrated the graph actor into PPO while retaining a conventional value network. The orthogonality term is optimized alongside the PPO objective, and the graph is batched across parallel ManiSkill environments.<br><br>This stage tested much more than a forward pass. Graph extraction, action alignment, stochastic action sampling, gradient flow, checkpointing, evaluation metrics, and video capture all had to work together. It established the engineering foundation for using ABD representations in another learning method.</blockquote></article>
<article class="script-step"><span class="script-time">3:15–4:20</span><h2>The imitation-learning extension</h2><div class="show-cue"><strong>Show:</strong> The diffusion-conditioning pipeline and the encoder-only ABD module.</div><blockquote class="script-copy">The paper places ABD-Net inside a reinforcement-learning actor. I wanted to test whether its body representation could also help a policy learn from demonstrations.<br><br>For diffusion policy, I removed ABD’s joint action decoder and kept only the link encoders and dynamics-informed message passing. Two state observations pass through an eight-link Panda arm graph. The resulting ABD features are concatenated with the unchanged raw state, and that combined representation conditions a one-dimensional U-Net while it denoises a sixteen-step action sequence.<br><br>The denoising gradient trains the U-Net, every per-link encoder, and the dynamics message-passing module together. The orthogonality loss continues to encourage the intended dynamics structure.</blockquote></article>
<article class="script-step"><span class="script-time">4:20–5:05</span><h2>Evaluation design</h2><div class="show-cue"><strong>Show:</strong> The dataset and evaluation settings.</div><blockquote class="script-copy">I evaluated the extension on ManiSkill’s RollBall task. The dataset contains 813 demonstration trajectories, 37,851 transitions, and 37,038 usable observation sequences.<br><br>Every main experiment trained for 100,000 iterations with batch size 1,024. Policies were evaluated every 5,000 iterations over 100 sparse-reward episodes. I compared the official diffusion policy against root-conditioned ABD at multiple action horizons, and then tested all-node variants to study how much structural information should be supplied to the diffusion model.</blockquote></article>
<article class="script-step"><span class="script-time">5:05–6:20</span><h2>What the results showed</h2><div class="show-cue"><strong>Show:</strong> The main comparison table and TensorBoard learning curves.</div><blockquote class="script-copy">The result is promising, but nuanced.<br><br>With an action horizon of eight, ABD and the official baseline both peak at 47 percent success. At horizon twelve, the baseline reaches 64 percent while ABD reaches 60 percent. However, ABD finishes at 59 percent while the baseline finishes at 44 percent.<br><br>At an action horizon of fifteen, ABD reaches 59 percent—four points above the baseline peak. This shows that the dynamics prior can be competitive and occasionally better, but the current evidence does not establish a universal improvement.<br><br>The all-node ablation produced another important result. A compact 256-dimensional condition reaches between 46 and 48 percent, while directly feeding all 2,048 ABD features peaks at 37 percent. More structural information is not automatically more useful. The way it is compressed and presented to the diffusion model matters.<br><br>Finally, success at the last episode step remains low across every method. The agent often reaches success transiently but does not reliably maintain it.</blockquote></article>
<article class="script-step"><span class="script-time">6:20–7:00</span><h2>Takeaway and next steps</h2><div class="show-cue"><strong>Show:</strong> Paper → implementation → PPO → diffusion → evaluation.</div><blockquote class="script-copy">This project creates an end-to-end path from a research idea to a tested extension. I reproduced the ABD computational prior, solved the simulator and graph-integration problems, validated it in reinforcement learning, and reused it as a structural encoder for imitation learning.<br><br>The next step is not to claim victory from one seed. It is to run matched multi-seed experiments, report confidence intervals, compare parameter-matched models, tune the structural regularization, test mass and friction shifts, and expand to contact-rich manipulation.<br><br>The central result is that ABD-Net does not have to remain an RL action head. It can act as a reusable, dynamics-grounded representation for other robot-learning methods.</blockquote></article>
</div></div></section>
